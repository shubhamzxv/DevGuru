<Analogy>

Cutting a **pizza**. Slices too big: nobody can hold one. Slices too small: just crumbs, no real bite. And if you cut **through** a topping, each slice gets half of it. **Chunking** is slicing documents: the right size, cut at natural lines, with a little **overlap** so nothing is lost at the edges.

</Analogy>

## Simple explanation

**Why chunk at all?**

- Embedding models have an **input limit**
- One vector for 50 pages is too vague to match a specific question
- You want to put only the **relevant part** into the prompt, not the whole document

**The trade-off**:

| Chunk size | Good | Bad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Small** (100–200 tokens) | precise matches | misses surrounding context |
| **Large** (800–1,500 tokens) | full context | vague embedding, more tokens, more noise |

A common starting point: **300–500 tokens with 10–20% overlap**, then measure and adjust.

**Overlap** repeats the end of one chunk at the start of the next, so a sentence cut at the border is still complete in one of them.

**Chunking strategies**:

| Strategy | How it cuts | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fixed size** | every N characters or tokens | quick start, messy text |
| **Recursive** | tries paragraphs, then sentences, then words, to stay under the size | the usual default |
| **Structure-aware** | by headings, sections, table rows, functions | Markdown, HTML, code, contracts |
| **Semantic** | cuts where the topic changes (embedding distance between sentences) | long mixed documents |
| **Parent-child** | search small chunks, return their larger parent section | precise search + full context |

**Always store metadata** with each chunk: source file, page or section title, date, language, access level. It lets you **filter** searches, show **citations**, and delete or update a document's chunks later.

**Tip**: adding the document title and section heading to the text of each chunk often improves retrieval, because a chunk like "It lasts 12 months" means little alone.

## Code example

Fixed-size chunking with overlap. See how a sentence gets cut, and how overlap keeps it whole in the next chunk:

```python
def fixed_chunks(text, size, overlap):
    step = size - overlap
    return [text[i:i + size] for i in range(0, len(text), step) if text[i:i + size].strip()]

text = ("Refunds take 7 working days. Returns are free within 10 days. "
        "Electronics have a 12 month warranty. Batteries are covered for 6 months.")

for chunk in fixed_chunks(text, size=60, overlap=0):
    print(repr(chunk))
print("--- with overlap 20 ---")
for chunk in fixed_chunks(text, size=60, overlap=20):
    print(repr(chunk))
```

```output
'Refunds take 7 working days. Returns are free within 10 days'
'. Electronics have a 12 month warranty. Batteries are covere'
'd for 6 months.'
--- with overlap 20 ---
'Refunds take 7 working days. Returns are free within 10 days'
' free within 10 days. Electronics have a 12 month warranty. '
' 12 month warranty. Batteries are covered for 6 months.'
'd for 6 months.'
```

Sentence-aware chunking: keep whole sentences, and pack them up to a size limit:

```python
import re

def sentence_chunks(text, max_chars, overlap_sentences=1):
    sentences = re.split(r"(?<=[.!?])\s+", text.strip())
    chunks, current = [], []
    for s in sentences:
        if current and len(" ".join(current + [s])) > max_chars:
            chunks.append(" ".join(current))
            current = current[-overlap_sentences:]          # carry the last sentence over
        current.append(s)
    chunks.append(" ".join(current))
    return chunks

text = ("Refunds take 7 working days. Returns are free within 10 days. "
        "Electronics have a 12 month warranty. Batteries are covered for 6 months. "
        "Contact support on chat for faster help.")

for i, chunk in enumerate(sentence_chunks(text, max_chars=75), 1):
    print(f"{i}. {chunk}")
```

```output
1. Refunds take 7 working days. Returns are free within 10 days.
2. Returns are free within 10 days. Electronics have a 12 month warranty.
3. Electronics have a 12 month warranty. Batteries are covered for 6 months.
4. Batteries are covered for 6 months. Contact support on chat for faster help.
```

Structure-aware chunking of Markdown, with **metadata** on every chunk:

```python
doc = """# Returns
Returns are free within 10 days.
# Warranty
Electronics have a 12 month warranty.
Batteries are covered for 6 months.
"""

chunks, title = [], None
for line in doc.strip().splitlines():
    if line.startswith("# "):
        title = line[2:]
        chunks.append({"text": "", "meta": {"source": "policy.md", "section": title}})
    else:
        chunks[-1]["text"] += (" " if chunks[-1]["text"] else "") + line

for c in chunks:
    c["text"] = f"{c['meta']['section']}: {c['text']}"       # put the heading into the chunk text
    print(c)
```

```output
{'text': 'Returns: Returns are free within 10 days.', 'meta': {'source': 'policy.md', 'section': 'Returns'}}
{'text': 'Warranty: Electronics have a 12 month warranty. Batteries are covered for 6 months.', 'meta': {'source': 'policy.md', 'section': 'Warranty'}}
```

<InterviewQA>

<QA q="Why is chunking needed in RAG?">

Embedding models have input limits, a single vector for a long document is too coarse to match specific questions, and the prompt should contain only relevant passages to save tokens and reduce noise. Chunking creates focused, retrievable units.

</QA>

<QA q="How do you choose chunk size and overlap?">

Start around 300 to 500 tokens with 10 to 20 percent overlap, then tune with a retrieval eval set. Short factual content suits smaller chunks; explanations and legal text need larger ones. Overlap protects information at boundaries at the cost of extra storage.

</QA>

<QA q="What chunking strategies do you know?">

Fixed-size, recursive splitting on a list of separators, structure-aware splitting by headings, sections, table rows or code units, semantic chunking at topic shifts, and parent-child (small-to-big) where small chunks are searched and their larger parents are sent to the model.

</QA>

<QA q="What metadata would you store with each chunk?">

Document id and source path or URL, page or section title, chunk position, timestamps and version, language, document type, and access-control tags. It enables filtering, citations, freshness rules and deleting or re-indexing a document.

</QA>
